## 2. Positioning

**The promise:** video editing for creators and filmmakers. We edit while you sleep, and everything is in writing.

- **Async and written by design.** It is a working style these clients accept, not an apology.
  - Briefs come in through a form.
  - Feedback comes as time-coded comments on a review link.
  - Messages are short and specific.
  - **Hera declines calls,** or turns them into a written process (Part 2 §6.8). Most competitors offer a written path
    to payment, though four still require a call for their biggest tiers (Part 3 §5).
- **Clear turnaround.** Every package states its turnaround in business days. The time zone means the work happens
  during the client's night; it does not mean next-day delivery for everything.
- **Fixed prices, published.** All 9 productized competitors publish prices (Part 3 §5). A fixed menu also removes the
  negotiation that is hardest without spoken English (Part 1).
- **AI-assisted, human-edited.**
  - AI tools do the first pass: transcripts, rough cuts, caption drafts, candidate moments.
  - The editor makes the choices.
  - Job posts reject "AI slop" and AI-written proposals, and some ban AI-generated visuals (Part 1). Platforms forbid
    AI use a client has restricted, and misrepresenting it (Part 4, answer 24).
- **The whole menu, sold niche by niche.** The pricing page lists every package. Each niche has its own page and its
  own samples.
- **The core business comes first.** In a busy filming week, close the queue to new one-off orders rather than miss a
  promised date.

## 3. Who to sell to

The editor does not speak English, so his spoken-English **listening** is the hidden constraint. Picking the best
moments of a podcast, a stream or a talking head means understanding what was said. Transcripts and translation help,
but they are slower. So the first tests favour segments with a low language load.

This departs from Part 1's ranking in two places:
- Part 1's third test, video podcasts, moves to the second wave because of its language load (Part 1: "medium to
  high").
- Part 1's fourth, white-label work for UK agencies, also waits. Agencies expect fast chat replies and occasional calls
  (Part 1), which an editor who writes but does not speak English cannot easily give at the start.

Keep the segments that pass, and drop the rest.

**What to avoid** (Part 1):
- **Per-view clipping.** The median is $1 per 1,000 views, and the average lifetime payout is about $30.
- **Per-clip work for small streamers.**
- **Comedy and commentary as a first niche.**
- **Coaches who expect calls.**
- **Corporate clients directly.** Reach them only through production companies.
- **EU clients before the VAT registration.**
- **Daily-turnaround promises.**
- **A Fiverr-first strategy.** Part 1 advises against it: about 27.7% of each order's value goes to Fiverr in fees from
  both sides, and its buyer numbers are falling. Part 3 nevertheless puts Fiverr first, because only the seller can
  start a call there. This plan follows Part 3, but runs YTJobs and direct outreach from week one, so Fiverr is never
  the only channel.

## 5. Packages and prices

Part 2 §6 has the full menu, with what each package includes, its limits, the add-ons and the hours behind each
price. The prices are **proposals to test**. The hours are estimates, so replace them with the editor's logged times
after the first 10 jobs.

**Principles** (Part 2 §6.1):
- **Rates.**
  - List prices aim at **$40–45 per editing hour**.
  - Ads, motion and corporate work carry $50–55.
  - The floor is **$35 for repeat work**; the founding price and the pilot below are one-off exceptions.
- **The Upwork benchmark.** The page puts video editors at "$10 – $60", median $35 an hour, with tier medians of $10,
  $40 and $100 ✓. The same page's body says "$6 - $25", as do Upwork's YouTube and real-estate pages.
- **Payment.** Packages are paid in advance. For custom quotes, new clients pay 50% upfront and the balance before the
  final files.
- **Revisions.** A revision round is one consolidated list of time-coded notes.
- **Turnaround.** Counted in business days. The clock starts when the footage, the brief and the payment are all in
  and the job reaches the front of the queue. Delays on the client's side pause it.
- **Music.** The client's licence, or a royalty-free library. Hera never resells music licences.
- **Fiverr prices.** Fiverr's seller fee is 20%, so once the first reviews are in, set a Fiverr price at the list
  price ÷ 0.8.

**Custom quotes** (Part 2 §6.8):
- **When:** corporate and event work, motion graphics, documentaries, footage over the package limits, rush jobs.
- **How:** a written intake form, then hours estimated from the footage and the finished minutes, plus 15% for
  revisions and 10% for admin.
- **Price:** $45 an hour standard; $55 for brands, ads, corporate and motion work; a minimum order of $120.
- **Turnaround on the quote:** sent within 1 business day, and valid for 14 days.

**Founding price for the first clients** (Part 2 §6.10, corrected):
- **The offer.** 30% off the first order only, for the first 10 clients or until 2027-03-31, whichever comes first. On
  Fiverr it can only be a public Basic tier, not a first-order discount.
- **No review condition.** The discount must **not** depend on a review. Fiverr prohibits "any attempt to manipulate
  reviews", which can mean permanent suspension. Upwork treats bribing or pressuring for feedback as feedback
  manipulation. Both points come from search summaries of the platforms' own pages, which block automated reading.
  Ask every client for honest feedback, with no conditions attached.
- **The paid pilot** ($99: one Clip Pack 10, one Talk edit or one podcast video episode, once per client) belongs to the
  second wave's monthly plans.
- **Test edits.** No free test edits beyond a 30–60-second sample on the client's own footage. YTJobs posts often ask
  for a test edit, usually unpaid: accept a short one (about an hour) for a serious post, and decline long unpaid tests.
- **When to raise prices.**
  - After 5 reviews averaging 4.8 or more: move to the list price.
  - After 15 reviews, or 4 weeks with the queue more than 80% full: raise prices by 10%.

**Capacity** (Part 2 §6.9; an estimate):
- **Billable hours.** 30 hours a week is about 130 hours a month. About 15% goes on messages, files and quotes,
  leaving about 110 billable hours.
- **The ceiling.** Full load at list prices is $4,400–4,600 gross; Part 2's example month is $4,550. After fees that
  is about $3,640 if every order came through Fiverr, or about $4,100 on Upwork at a 10% fee (its fee is 0–15%).
- **The example month** mixes in second-wave work, but the first-wave packages earn a similar $40–49 an hour.
- **Retainers.** At most 2 monthly plans at once, so one-off orders keep their delivery dates.

## 9. Legal, tax and money

This is research, not advice; Part 4 §8 has the full list for the lawyer and accountant.

**Before the first foreign purchase, gig or invoice (accountant: A1, A2, A3):**
- **The company form.**
  - An ЕООД pays 10% corporate tax plus 5% on dividends, about 14.5% on distributed profit, with limited liability.
  - An ЕТ pays **15%** on business profit (ЗДДФЛ art. 48(2) ✓; corrected from Part 4's 10%), with unlimited liability.
  - So the ЕООД is no worse on tax.
- **Whose name the accounts are in.** Marketplace accounts and payouts should be in the company's name once it
  exists. Ask the accountant whether to wait for registration or start in the business owner's name, so personal and
  company income are not mixed.
- **VAT for EU business clients.** Selling to a business in another EU country requires a VAT registration before
  the first supply, whatever the turnover (ЗДДС art. 97а(2) ✓). The application is due at least 7 days before the
  advance payment or completion (art. 97а(4) ✓). The one exception, since 2026, is registration for the EU
  small-enterprise scheme in that country (art. 97а(6) ✓).
  - With that registration, the 20% reverse-charge VAT on Fiverr and Upwork fees and on foreign software becomes a
    real cost, because it cannot be deducted.
  - Buying those services from abroad may itself trigger registration (art. 97а(1)), which is why A2 comes before
    the first such purchase.
- **US, UK, Canadian and Australian business clients** are outside the scope of Bulgarian VAT. Treat a client as a
  business only with proof (a business name, registration or VAT number); otherwise treat them as a consumer.
- **The €51,130 threshold.** Sales to businesses abroad do not count toward it. Bulgarian filming does, and so do
  sales to consumers; for consumers outside the EU that is Part 4's reading, for the accountant to confirm (A4).
- **Invoices.** They can be in USD or GBP, with the tax base also shown in euro at the ECB rate. Issue them within 5
  days, with the right wording for reverse charge or out-of-scope supplies.
- **US forms.** The company gives US payers and platforms a W-8BEN-E; an ЕТ would give a W-8BEN. These forms are given
  to the payer, not filed. Editing done in Bulgaria is not US-source income, so nothing is withheld.

**Before the first contract (lawyer: L1, L2):**
- **Copyright.**
  - Whether an editor is an author of the finished film is unsettled. Art. 62 names a film's director, screenwriter
    and director of photography (L1).
  - So use two written grants: the editor to the company, then the company to the client.
  - Without a contract, the client may use the work only for the purpose it was ordered for (art. 42).
  - A written grant must state exclusivity, term and territory. If it leaves them out, the defaults are
    non-exclusive, 3 years and the client's country (art. 36). Attribution rights cannot be given up.
  - For US clients, Part 4 suggests "work made for hire" plus a fallback assignment. That is its own reading, for the
    lawyer to confirm.
- **Data protection.**
  - Editing footage of people makes Hera a processor, so each client needs a data processing agreement.
  - It must list the cloud, review and AI tools used as sub-processors, which the client must authorise.
  - Clients outside the EU, the UK and Canada need the standard contractual clauses (Module 4), and so do US clients
    not certified under the Data Privacy Framework, which is most creators and filmmakers.
  - Opt out of Upwork's default licence to use work for AI training.
- **Terms for the packages.** Scope, footage limits, revisions, turnaround, refunds, liability caps (Part 4 §4.6).

**Before the site's own checkout (accountant: A6):**
- **The e-shop rule.** Selling packages by card on the company's own site likely makes it an "e-shop" under Наредба
  Н-18 (Part 4's reading). It must then be registered with НАП before sales, and card payments need fiscal receipts or
  the alternative document.
- **Sell to businesses only at first.** That avoids the consumer rules: the 14-day withdrawal right, the withdrawal
  button required since 24.09.2026 (ЗЗП art. 52а), and the threshold count.

**Cold outreach rules** (Part 4 §5):

| Country | Rule |
|---|---|
| US | Allowed, with identification and an opt-out |
| UK | Allowed to companies and LLPs; sole traders and partnerships need consent |
| Canada and Australia | Consent needed, but it can be implied from a conspicuously published business address plus a relevant message |
| Germany | Consent needed, even between businesses |

X, Instagram and Discord ban bulk, automated or repeated commercial messages, so treat DMs like email: one-to-one,
relevant, identified, with an opt-out.

**Money tools.**
- Wise Business for USD and GBP receipts: €60 to set up, local receipts free.
- Stripe for cards, after A6.
- The marketplaces' own payouts: Fiverr to PayPal $0; Upwork to a local bank $0.99.

## 10. Risks

| Risk | What reduces it |
|---|---|
| AI tools take the simple work | Sell judgment: story, structure, wedding pacing, real-estate polish. Use AI for the first pass |
| Offshore price floors ($35–80 a video) | Compete on quality and reliability, not price; avoid volume niches without proof |
| English comprehension in dialogue-heavy footage | Low-language niches first; transcripts plus translation; test on a sample before accepting podcast or stream clients |
| Written-only communication puts some clients off | Choose segments that brief in writing; a clear FAQ and fixed menu; fast written replies |
| Marketplace dependence: fees, rule changes, falling buyers | YTJobs and direct outreach from week one; the site from weeks 4–8 |
